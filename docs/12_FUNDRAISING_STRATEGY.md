# 12 — Fundraising and investor red team
## One-sentence pitch
Bharosa is an India-first digital task-completion layer that helps users safely finish unfamiliar app and phone workflows and could reduce support friction for institutions that sponsor access.

## 60-second pitch
People frequently fail at unfamiliar digital workflows and are pushed toward costly phone support or unsafe strangers. Bharosa starts with six reviewed, bilingual/tri-lingual device-specific paths, understandable step progression, honest outcome reporting and user-controlled human help. Our hypothesis is that banks, telecoms and service providers will pay for fewer unresolved journeys. We are not claiming proof yet: the next milestones are task-completion uplift, four-week repeat value, and paid pilot contracts. We will not automate movement of money or monetise urgent fraud reporting.

## Three-minute narrative
Show a senior user choosing device and task, completing reviewed steps without sharing secrets; when stuck, previewing the only outbound message and explicitly opening a WhatsApp draft. Contrast with AI chat volume: the metric is verified task completion, not conversations. Show institutional pilot design with independent comparison of support minutes and error rates. Separate historical code audits, new committed features and currently unverified browser/CI output. Explain business model: hypothesised per-eligible-seat fee, low-cost deterministic flows, variable human escalation; downside is partner sales friction, consumer platform duplication and unmeasured willingness-to-pay. End with explicit 30/60/90/180 acceptance gates instead of valuation theatre.

## Hypothetical financing
**No raise has been approved or solicited.** A pre-seed planning range might be ₹1.5–3 crore conditional on pilot economics and product/security completion; actual size, instrument, equity and dilution require financing strategy and runway model with founder approval. Budget priority: product QA/security 35%, localized guides 15%, pilot and user research 25%, institutional integrations 15%, contingency 10%. These are planning allocations, not commitments.

## Ten objections and evidence-based answers
1. Why can't Google do it? — They can; differentiator must become partner-specific completed outcomes.
2. Who pays? — Proposed sponsor; no signed payer yet.
3. Why return? — Recurring tasks hypothesised; repeat cohort study required.
4. Is it just scam detection? — No; scam check is a free gateway.
5. Does it handle finance? — It never requests PIN, OTP, banking credentials or moves money.
6. Is WhatsApp escalation automatic? — No; manual user-sent draft.
7. What is CAC? — Scenario estimate only; measure fully-loaded acquisition for 2 pilots.
8. Why won't models hallucinate? — Common task flows are curated; scam evaluation remains model-dependent.
9. What is TAM? — Scenario seat capacity; sponsor willingness-to-pay/reachable share still unknown.
10. Why invest today? — Presently the evidence supports a pilot programme, not a priced VC round.

## Investor-ready milestone
Pass independent CI/device QA + security, show ≥20pp controlled task completion improvement, ≥25% weekly repeat-value hypothesis, ≥2 paying institutional pilots, audited cost per verified task and privacy/legal review. Only then replace a pilot deck with a fundraising deck.
